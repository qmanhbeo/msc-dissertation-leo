Topic 1: Mapping Fair Reinforcement Learning for Resource Allocation: A Citation Network and NLP-Based Topic modelling Analysis
Motivation
Resource allocation lies at the core of economics and sustainable development, as it determines how scarce resources are distributed under constraints of uncertainty, efficiency, and equity. Reinforcement learning (RL) has increasingly been proposed for dynamic allocation problems, including smart grids (Zhang et al., 2018), humanitarian logistics (Yu et al., 2021), disaster response (Van Steenbergen et al., 2023), etc.
A central insight from this MSc is that machine learning systems optimized purely for performance can reproduce or amplify inequality. Allocation systems are particularly sensitive because they determine access to critical resources. In RL, the reward function defines what the system learns to optimize, making it a natural site for incorporating fairness constraints.
Despite rapid growth in this area, the structural organization of fair RL for resource allocation remains unclear. It is unknown how application domains cluster, how fairness is operationalized across methods, and whether technical optimization research is integrated with sustainability and governance concerns.
Research questions
•	What structural clusters define fair RL for resource allocation research (2022–2026)?
•	How is fairness operationalized across methodological streams?
•	Are theoretical (e.g., RMAB) and deep RL approaches integrated or fragmented?
•	Which application domains dominate the field?
•	What recurring methodological or governance gaps appear across clusters?
Method
•	Collect corpus using OpenAlex / Scopus.
•	Construct citation network and apply community detection (e.g., Louvain).
•	Generate embeddings (Sentence-BERT).
•	Apply topic modeling (e.g., BERTopic).
•	Overlay semantic themes onto citation clusters.
•	Analyze centrality, fragmentation, and thematic distribution.
Contribution
This study provides a structural and semantic mapping of fair reinforcement learning for resource allocation, identifying dominant paradigms, fragmentation patterns, and underexplored intersections between fairness, sustainability, and governance.

Topic 2: Semantic Alignment Between AI Sustainability Research and Policy Frameworks
Core Question
To what extent does academic AI-for-sustainability research align with sustainability policy priorities?
Motivation
AI governance emphasizes that technical development and policy objectives must co-evolve. However, academic AI research and sustainability policy documents may prioritize different themes. Misalignment could lead to technically sophisticated systems that fail to address real-world sustainability needs.
Topic modelling has been used to map AI and ML applications to SDGs in the academic literature (Nedungadi et al., 2024; Raman et al., 2024). Theodorou et al. (2022) use keyword-based text mining (TF-IDF) to analyze national AI strategies and finds limited and uneven references to the SDGs. This means, according to my knowledge, no study has applied topic modelling to map out the possible mismatch between academic literature and government/IO policy documents in the evolving application of AI into SDGs.
Method
•	Collect AI-for-sustainability research abstracts (OpenAlex / Scopus).
•	Collect sustainability policy documents (e.g., UN SDGs, national AI strategies, climate action reports).
•	Generate embeddings (Sentence-BERT).
•	Measure semantic similarity (cosine similarity).
•	Apply topic modeling to both corpora.
•	Compare thematic prominence and divergence.
•	Identify underrepresented policy themes in research literature.
Contribution
Provides a quantitative assessment of alignment between AI research and sustainability governance priorities, revealing thematic gaps and structural mismatches.
Reference
Nedungadi, P., Surendran, S., Tang, K. Y., & Raman, R. (2024). Big data and AI algorithms for sustainable development goals: a topic modeling analysis. Ieee Access, 12, 188519-188541. https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2024.3516500
Raman, R., Pattnaik, D., Lathabai, H. H., Kumar, C., Govindan, K., & Nedungadi, P. (2024). Green and sustainable AI research: an integrated thematic and topic modeling analysis. Journal of Big Data, 11(1), 1-28. https://doi.org/10.1186/s40537-024-00920-x
